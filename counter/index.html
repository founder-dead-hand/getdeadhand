The App
For the Counter

Something worth handing over.

I'm a plumber. I built a quoting tool for the guys standing at your counter, and I'd like them to know it exists.

I run Tom Falk Plumbing & Heating. A while back I built Dead Hand because I kept losing money at the kitchen table — tired, customer waiting, and the number I said in the next thirty seconds was the one I had to honor in the morning.

You know the guys I mean. One truck, maybe three. Good at the work. They price in their head and most of them are better at it than they think, but not at seven on a Friday night. Some of them are underwater on jobs they would swear made money.

Dead Hand fixes the number, not the man. They set their labor rates, their material margin and their overhead once. After that they put in the hours and the parts and the right price comes out with the margin already inside it. Then they turn the phone around and the customer gets a clean estimate with none of that on it. Twelve ninety-nine a month. It runs in a browser, so there is no app store and nothing to install on whatever phone they already carry.

Built for the small shop, not the enterprise.

The big field-service platforms are built for a company with a dispatcher, an office and an onboarding budget. That's a real business and they serve it well. It just isn't the man at your counter with one truck and a helper.

I'm not trying to get in the truck with everyone who works for every company. I'm trying to get in the truck with the ones it will actually help. That's why it's priced by the size of the shop instead of by the head, and why there's no implementation package. A one-man shop doesn't need onboarding. He needs the right number at the kitchen table.

What it does that the flat-rate books don't

The numbers are his. The flat-rate books sell a maintained price list and update it every quarter. Dead Hand ships the shape of a job with every price blank — his town, his overhead, his payroll, not somebody else's.
He isn't charged by the head. Per-tech pricing costs a shop more every time it hires. This is flat by the size of the shop.
Nothing to set up. No implementation fee, no onboarding package, no consultant sitting in his kitchen.
The price is on the website. Nobody has to sit through a demo to find out what it costs.
Margin on every line while he builds the price — before he says the number, not after the job is done.

$12.99 a month for the calculator, or $45 to $180 flat by shop size for the whole book of jobs priced and ready. The cheapest per-tech book runs about three times the calculator for a single man, plus a few hundred dollars before he quotes anything.

No spiff, and that's on purpose.

I'm not offering you a cut. Plenty of houses don't let their people take money from a vendor, and I'm not going to hand you something you'd have to declare or turn down.

The honest version is simpler: someone doing better work and making more money is a better customer for you. The ones who guess low are the ones who quietly disappear owing you money.

What it isn't

It isn't a CRM and it isn't going to turn into one. It doesn't take payments, doesn't chase invoices, and doesn't do anything with a customer list. It prices a job and hands over an estimate. If one of your contractors already runs software that does the rest, this sits next to it and doesn't argue.

And I don't sell them prices. Every number in there is theirs. I'm not putting a parts catalog in front of your customers with my markup on it — that's your business, not mine.
