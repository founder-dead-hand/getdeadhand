The pocket tool for field quoting

I kept losing money at the kitchen table — tired, customer waiting, and the number I said in the next thirty seconds was the one I had to honor in the morning. So I built a quoting tool for that moment.

I’m a plumber. I’ve run a plumbing and heating shop for years, and I built this for my own truck first. Dead Hand sets a shop’s labor rate, material margin and overhead once, then does the number on the spot and hands the customer a clean estimate. $15 a month, in a browser — no app store, no install, no demo call.

And I don’t sell them prices.

Every number in there is the contractor’s own. I ship the shape of a job with every price blank and I keep no parts catalog. I am not putting my markup on your material in front of your customers. That’s your business, not mine.

No spiff, and that’s on purpose.

I’m not offering you a cut. Plenty of houses don’t let their people take money from a vendor, and I’m not going to hand you something you’d have to declare or turn down.

Why it’s worth the counter space

You carry these guys on terms. The ones who guess low are the ones who quietly disappear owing you money — not because they’re bad at the work, but because they priced a job in their head at seven on a Friday and ate the difference all year.

Someone doing better work and making more money is a better customer for you. That’s the whole argument.
